EMI bounced once? Here is what can happen to your loan and credit score

Your EMI was due, but the auto-debit failed. It could be because your salary had not arrived, the account balance was low, or there was a problem with the bank mandate. Whatever the reason, the first thing to do is clear the unpaid amount rather than waiting for the next debit attempt.

A single bounced EMI does not automatically turn a loan into a bad account or cause a fixed number of points to disappear from your CIBIL score. The real problem starts when the payment remains overdue and the lender reports the delay to credit information companies.

A failed EMI can mean extra charges

The immediate impact is usually on your pocket. Depending on the lender and the loan agreement, you may have to pay a bounce or return charge along with the overdue EMI. Interest may also continue to apply until the amount is paid.

RBI rules require lenders to disclose penal charges and the reason for imposing them. Such charges cannot be added to the loan as penal interest and then capitalised for further interest. The actual charge, however, varies from one lender to another.

Suppose your monthly EMI is Rs. 18,000 and the debit fails. Do not assume that paying Rs. 18,000 a few days later will necessarily settle everything. Check your loan account and ask the lender for the exact amount outstanding before making the payment.

Your credit score does not fall by a fixed number

There is no standard RBI formula saying that one bounced EMI will reduce your CIBIL score by, say, 20 or 50 points. Credit scores are calculated from the information in your credit report, and payment history is one of the factors considered by CIBIL.

What matters is how the missed payment appears in the report. Credit reports use Days Past Due, or DPD, to show how late a payment was. A DPD of 000 means there are no payments past due, while a number such as 030 indicates 30 days past due.

So, paying the EMI quickly is very different from leaving it unpaid for a month or longer. Your lender's reporting cycle also matters. RBI now requires regulated entities to submit credit information more frequently, on a fortnightly basis under the current reporting framework.

The longer you wait, the more serious it gets

A bounced EMI by itself does not make a bank loan an NPA. RBI's classification rules generally treat a term loan as an NPA when the principal or interest remains overdue for more than 90 days.

Before that stage, accounts can move through different Special Mention Account categories based on the length of the overdue period. SMA-0 covers overdue amounts up to 30 days, SMA-1 covers more than 30 days and up to 60 days, and SMA-2 covers more than 60 days and up to 90 days.

For a borrower, the message is fairly simple. Do not let one failed EMI become the next missed EMI.

What should you do after the bounce?

First, find out why the payment failed. If there was not enough money in the account, arrange the funds and make the payment through the method confirmed by the lender. Keep the receipt or transaction reference.

If the failure happened because of a technical problem or an incorrect debit, complain to the lender and keep copies of the communication. Do not assume that the problem will correct itself.

After the payment is made, check your loan account to make sure the overdue amount has been cleared. You can also check your credit report later. CIBIL says payment updates from lenders can take around 15 to 30 days to appear in the report.

One bounced EMI is usually a manageable problem. Leaving it unattended is what can make it expensive, and potentially turn a temporary cash shortage into a problem that follows your credit history.
